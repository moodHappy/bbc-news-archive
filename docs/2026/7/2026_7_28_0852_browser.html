A Japanese town wrestles with identity after protests over its first mosque

📅 2026-07-28 08:52 🔗 阅读原文 🔙 返回日历 📡 同步中...

Today, it's a field of grass. But soon, it'll be the site of the first mosque in this Japanese town.

今天，这里是一片草地。但很快，这里将成为这个日本小镇第一座清真寺的所在地。

"It makes me worry about how our community might change," says long-time resident Noriko Izumizawa.

“这让我担心我们的社区可能会发生什么变化，”长期居民泉泽纪子说。

Plans to build an Islamic religious site in Fujisawa, a laid-back seaside town about an hour's drive south of Tokyo, have sparked a wide range of emotions.

在东京以南约一小时车程的休闲海滨小镇藤泽建立伊斯兰宗教场所的计划引发了广泛的情绪。

There is anger and fear, as well as hope and acceptance, exposing the growing anxieties over immigration and social change as Japan grapples with a labour shortage from a shrinking and ageing population.

在日本努力应对人口萎缩和老龄化造成的劳动力短缺之际，愤怒和恐惧、希望和接受，暴露了人们对移民和社会变革日益增长的焦虑。

Although immigration remains politically sensitive and restrictive, Japan is increasingly relying on foreign workers to fill open jobs. That means an influx of immigrants with various cultures, languages, religions and ways of being.

尽管移民仍然具有政治敏感性和限制性，但日本越来越依赖外国工人来填补空缺职位。这意味着具有不同文化、语言、宗教和生活方式的移民涌入。

So the debate that is playing out in towns like Fujisawa across the country is one that goes to the heart of how Japan sees itself.

因此，在全国各地像藤泽这样的城镇上演的争论是一场触及日本如何看待自己的核心问题的争论。

Izumizawa is not alone in the way she feels. She is among a group of Fujisawa residents who are anxious about the increasingly visible Muslim community. She says that the polarising debates around immigration in the US and Europe have also contributed to her unease.

泉泽并不孤单有这种感觉。她是藤泽居民中的一员，他们对日益明显的穆斯林社区感到焦虑。她说，美国和欧洲围绕移民问题的两极分化辩论也加剧了她的不安。

Earlier this year, anti-mosque protests flared up at the local train station, where thousands took to the streets to object to the proposed construction of the mosque. Some argue that a mosque much larger than the nearby Japanese Shinto shrine is disrespectful.

今年早些时候，当地火车站爆发了反清真寺抗议活动，数千人走上街头反对修建清真寺的提议。一些人认为，一座比附近的日本神社大得多的清真寺是不尊重的。

There have also been reports of hate speech and misinformation spreading on Japanese social media. Local outlets say mosques have been receiving abusive phone calls and emails, some of which has prompted anti-hate groups to organise counter protests calling for no hate.

还有报道称，仇恨言论和错误信息在日本社交媒体上传播。当地媒体称，清真寺不断收到辱骂电话和电子邮件，其中一些促使反仇恨团体组织反抗议活动，呼吁不要仇恨。

Hiroki Suzuka, who manages a coffee shop opposite the road where the Fujisawa mosque is set to be built, calls for greater understanding and dialogue.

铃鹿弘树 (Hiroki Suzuka) 在即将修建藤泽清真寺的道路对面经营一家咖啡店，他呼吁加强理解和对话。

"I'm not particularly opposed, but I wouldn't say I'm strongly in favour either," Suzuka said.

“我并不是特别反对，但我也不会说我强烈支持，”铃鹿说。

"I think the first step is to learn about them, what Islam is, what kind of people they are, and what values and perspectives they hold. It begins with having an open mind and a desire to understand each other."

“我认为第一步是了解他们，了解伊斯兰教是什么，他们是什么样的人，以及他们持有什么价值观和观点。首先要有开放的心态和相互理解的愿望。”

Being Muslim in Japan

Businessman Mohamed Mohideen has lived in Osaka for the past 39 years. He moved from his home country Sri Lanka in 1987 for better work opportunities during the economic bubble.

商人穆罕默德·莫希丁 (Mohamed Mohiden) 已在大阪生活了 39 年。 1987 年，他在经济泡沫时期从祖国斯里兰卡移居，寻求更好的工作机会。

He has dealt gemstones and later worked in trade, exporting used cars to countries like Sri Lanka and Pakistan.

他从事过宝石交易，后来从事贸易工作，向斯里兰卡和巴基斯坦等国家出口二手车。

He moved at a time, he says, when there were far fewer foreign residents.

他说，他搬家的时候，外国居民还少得多。

But Mohideen feels that compared to back then, local people have become hostile and started making hurtful remarks at Muslims, both online and in person.

但莫希丁认为，与那时相比，当地人已经变得充满敌意，并开始在网上和当面发表对穆斯林的伤害性言论。

"On social media, there are countless comments like, 'Get out of Japan' and 'Go back to your own country,'" Mohideen said. "People have also started coming to the mosque and shouting abuse."

“社交媒体上有无数的评论，比如‘滚出日本’和‘回到你自己的国家’，”莫希丁说。 “人们也开始来到清真寺大声辱骂。”

Mohideen goes to the Osaka mosque every day for prayers and other duties as a member of the local Islamic community. He says he has never felt that his life was in danger.

作为当地伊斯兰社区的一员，莫希丁每天都会去大阪清真寺进行祈祷和履行其他职责。他说他从未感到自己的生命有危险。

Despite having good relationships with neighbours for many years, Mohideen has noticed the shift in attitude towards Muslims more recently.

尽管多年来与邻居保持着良好的关系，但莫希丁最近注意到人们对穆斯林的态度发生了转变。

"Now, I feel that people are afraid of us."

“现在，我觉得人们害怕我们。”

The verbal abuse has worsened in recent years. He recalled members of his Muslim community being called terrorists, accused of making bombs, and being told to get out of Japan.

近年来，言语虐待现象愈演愈烈。他回忆说，他的穆斯林社区成员被称为恐怖分子，被指控制造炸弹，并被告知离开日本。

"When that happens, the only thing we can do is be patient. They're trying to provoke a fight or create a confrontation. They want to see how long we'll tolerate it," Mohideen explained.

“当这种情况发生时，我们唯一能做的就是保持耐心。他们试图挑起战斗或制造对抗。他们想看看我们能容忍多久，”莫希丁解释道。

"So we stay calm, speak gently, and treat them kindly, because Japanese people generally don't know much about Islam."

“所以我们保持冷静，说话温和，善待他们，因为日本人普遍对伊斯兰教了解不多。”

Still, he thinks there is some shared responsibility for the rift.

不过，他认为双方的裂痕有一些共同的责任。

"We focused only on our work and, for a long time, didn't make enough effort to help people understand who we are. We also didn't do enough to become part of the society," he said.

“长期以来，我们只专注于我们的工作，没有做出足够的努力来帮助人们了解我们是谁。我们也没有做足够的努力来成为社会的一部分，”他说。

"From now on, I want to work harder to increase dialogue and build better mutual understanding."

“从现在开始，我想更加努力地加强对话，建立更好的相互理解。”

Shakeel Mohamed, 61, has been representing the Yashio mosque in Saitama prefecture for the past decade. He had worked as a construction site supervisor for 26 years after moving to Japan from Pakistan and speaks fluent Japanese.

61 岁的沙基尔·穆罕默德 (Shakeel Mohamed) 在过去十年中一直代表埼玉县八潮清真寺。从巴基斯坦移居日本后，他曾担任建筑工地主管26年，能说一口流利的日语。

Mohamed says the most important thing is to follow Japan's rules, including sorting refuse properly, obeying parking laws, and wearing helmets when riding your bicycle.

穆罕默德说，最重要的是遵守日本的规则，包括正确分类垃圾、遵守停车法以及骑自行车时戴头盔。

"We make every effort to respect the way things are done in Japan and to follow the rules," he said. "Courtesy is important too, even something as simple as greeting people."

“我们尽一切努力尊重日本的行事方式并遵守规则，”他说。 “礼貌也很重要，即使是像打招呼这样简单的事情。”

He said Japanese locals in Yashio refer to the area's Pakistani community as "Yashio-stan".

他说，八潮的日本当地人将该地区的巴基斯坦社区称为“八潮斯坦”。

"Of course, there are some Pakistanis who behave badly, but they're only a small minority. And the same is true of Japanese people."

“当然，也有一些巴基斯坦人表现不好，但那只是极少数。日本人也是如此。”

Immigration vs identity

Japan is wrestling with its identity in the face of huge demographic shifts.

面对巨大的人口变化，日本正在努力解决自己的身份问题。

Muslims in Japan, including foreign residents and Japanese believers, numbered roughly 420,000 at the end of 2024, up from 230,000 in 2019, according to Hirofumi Tanada, a professor emeritus at Waseda University who studies the faith in Japan.

研究日本信仰的早稻田大学名誉教授田田博文表示，截至 2024 年底，包括外国居民和日本信徒在内的日本穆斯林人数约为 42 万人，高于 2019 年的 23 万人。

The country's growing Muslim population also reflects a greater nationwide trend. The foreign resident population – which mainly comes from China, Vietnam, South Korea and the Philippines - surpassed four million in 2025. A record 2.57 million of them work in Japan, according to government data. In the same year, Japan's population declined by nearly 1 million.

该国穆斯林人口的增长也反映了更大的全国趋势。外国居民人口（主要来自中国、越南、韩国和菲律宾）到 2025 年将超过 400 万。根据政府数据，其中 257 万人在日本工作，创历史新高。同年，日本人口减少了近100万。

Government officials have long avoided describing the country as an immigration country, but push visas that are seen as more temporary than permanent. Critics describe this as a "stealth" immigration system.

政府官员长期以来一直避免将该国描述为移民国家，但推行被视为临时而非永久的签证。批评者将此描述为“隐形”移民系统。

But even then, Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi has adopted a highly conservative stance against large-scale immigration. In June 2026, the Japanese government decided to raise visa fees for foreign nationals fivefold, after not having increased the amount for nearly 50 years.

但即便如此，首相高市早苗仍对大规模移民采取高度保守的立场。 2026年6月，日本政府决定将外国人签证费提高五倍，此前该金额已近50年没有提高。

Now, the government is planning to increase the number of employees at the Immigration Services Agency by about 230 people, aiming to strengthen enforcement against foreigners illegally overstaying. Officials may also introduce a limit to manage the number of foreign residents in the country.

目前，政府正计划将入国管理局员工人数增加约230人，旨在加强对外国人非法逾期居留的执法力度。官员们还可能会实行限制来管理该国的外国居民人数。

As immigrant communities become more visible, the country is being forced to confront a question it has long tried to avoid: Can Japan become a more diverse country while preserving its sense of identity and cohesion that it values?

随着移民社区变得更加明显，该国被迫面对一个长期以来一直试图避免的问题：日本能否成为一个更加多元化的国家，同时保留其所珍视的认同感和凝聚力？

Back in Fujisawa, Izumizawa admits she has never had a negative experience with a Muslim neighbour herself.

回到藤泽后，泉泽承认她自己从未与穆斯林邻居有过不好的经历。

"The truth is, maybe I just fear the unknown," she shrugs.

“事实是，也许我只是害怕未知，”她耸耸肩。
