On the front line of Ecuador's drugs war, police fight gangs, guns and corruption

📅 2026-07-28 13:13 🔗 阅读原文 🔙 返回日历 📡 同步中...

In the Ecuadoran city of Durán, riddled with drugs and rival gangs, Police Captain Jean Carlos Bustamante knows exactly what he is up against – not just the criminals who kill and sometimes dismember their rivals, but also the enemy within.

在厄瓜多尔杜兰市，毒品泛滥，敌对帮派猖獗，警察队长让·卡洛斯·布斯塔曼特清楚地知道自己面临的是什么——不仅是杀害甚至肢解对手的罪犯，还有内部的敌人。

"We have many, many bad people inside our unit, inside the state, inside politics," he says. "We have many, many guys that are corrupt. They are in the pay of the gangs."

“我们单位内部、国家内部、政治内部有很多很多坏人，”他说。 “我们有很多很多腐败分子。他们受黑帮的贿赂。”

The only police officers he can trust, he says, are the ones right beside him - members of his hand-picked team. At 34, Captain Bustamante is the oldest among them and moves like the special forces officer he used to be.

他说，他唯一可以信任的警察就是他身边的警察——他精心挑选的团队成员。 34 岁的布斯塔曼特上尉是他们中年龄最大的，他的动作就像他以前的特种部队军官一样。

With the sun setting, he leads his team into a hardscrabble neighbourhood where windows are sealed off behind metal grills, and lips are sealed by fear. This is the heart of gangland, controlled by a group called Los Aguilas (Spanish for The Eagles).

夕阳西下，他带领他的团队进入一个贫困的社区，那里的窗户被金属格栅封住，人们因恐惧而闭嘴。 这里是黑社会的中心，由一个名为“Los Aguilas”（西班牙语为“老鹰”）的组织控制。

I ask what happens if local people speak out against the gangs. "They kill them," the captain replies. "So that's why they never say nothing."

我问如果当地人公开反对这些团伙，会发生什么。 “他们杀了他们，”船长回答道。 “所以这就是为什么他们从不说什么。”

He's heading for an abandoned house where he believes weapons have been hidden (gunmen don't keep their guns at home). He and his team line up, hugging the wall, heavily armed, and tightly packed. A battering ram gets them through the door.

他正前往一栋废弃的房子，他认为那里藏有武器（枪手不会把枪留在家里）。他和他的团队紧靠墙列队，全副武装，挤得严严实实。攻城锤将他们带进了门。

They find two handguns – one rusty but the other ready to be fired - and ammunition for an assault rifle.

他们发现了两把手枪——一把生锈了，但另一把随时可以开火——以及突击步枪的弹药。

The captain says he's happy with the result. "The first reason is because we are safe," he says. "The second is that there are two guns less for the gangs."

队长说他对结果很满意。 “第一个原因是因为我们很安全，”他说。 “第二是帮派少了两把枪。”

But this is a small victory, in a war of attrition. He admits the gangs have "many weapons and much more money than the state".

但这只是一场消耗战中的小胜利。他承认这些团伙拥有“比国家更多的武器和更多的钱”。

He knows the risks only too well – having lost three close friends fighting the gangs. He has also lost his fear he says, "years ago", but his parents have not.

他非常清楚其中的风险——在与帮派的斗争中失去了三个亲密的朋友。他说，“几年前”，他也失去了恐惧，但他的父母还没有。

"My Mum says 'be careful, maybe you can die there'," he tells me. "My Dad calls all the time and asks 'my son, are you good? Tell me that you are good.'"

“我妈妈说‘小心，也许你会死在那里’，”他告诉我。 “我爸爸总是打电话问‘我的儿子，你好吗？告诉我你好吗。’”

A line can be traced - in dollars and in broken lives - from the back streets of Durán to the hotspots of London, and other capital cities. Around 70% of the cocaine reaching Europe and the US is funnelled out of Ecuador, according to the country's president, Daniel Noboa.

从杜兰的后街到伦敦和其他首都城市的热点地区，可以用金钱和破碎的生活来追踪一条线。据厄瓜多尔总统丹尼尔·诺博亚 (Daniel Noboa) 称，运往欧洲和美国的可卡因大约 70% 是从厄瓜多尔流出的。

How did it come to this?

怎么会变成这样呢？

The drug pours in from neighbouring Colombia and Peru – the two biggest producers in the world. Powerful home-grown gangs – with links to Mexican cartels and Albanian mafia groups – help smuggle the cocaine onwards. They take advantage of Ecuador's weak border controls, underfunded security forces, and long Pacific coast.

这种毒品从邻国哥伦比亚和秘鲁——世界上最大的两个生产国——涌入。强大的本土帮派——与墨西哥卡特尔和阿尔巴尼亚黑手党集团有联系——帮助走私可卡因。他们利用厄瓜多尔薄弱的边境管制、资金不足的安全部队以及漫长的太平洋海岸。

In the process what was a peaceful tourist haven in South America has – in under a decade - become a killing ground.

在此过程中，南美原本宁静的旅游天堂在不到十年的时间里变成了杀戮场。

Last year the murder rate was one of the highest in the world, with more than 9,200 people killed, according to Ecuador's interior ministry. That's despite an iron-fisted crackdown by President Noboa over the past two years. Critics say it has backfired. Top crime bosses have been arrested, causing the gangs to fracture, but also to multiply.

据厄瓜多尔内政部称，去年谋杀率是世界上最高的之一，有 9,200 多人被杀。尽管诺博亚总统在过去两年进行了铁腕镇压，但情况仍然如此。批评人士称，此举适得其反。犯罪头目已被逮捕，导致犯罪团伙瓦解，但数量也不断增加。

Many here believe the war against the gangs will never be won.

这里的许多人相信，打击帮派的战争永远不会获胜。

Captain Bustamante keeps fighting.

布斯塔曼特队长继续战斗。

When we join him for another operation he's lying in wait getting a bird's eye view of the back alleys from a drone. He sees three figures moving around at the edge of a playground – "micro-traffickers" - caught in the act, he says. They are at the bottom of the drug chain, selling locally, in minute amounts, not kilos.

当我们和他一起进行另一次行动时，他正在等待，从无人机上鸟瞰后巷。他说，他看到三个人影在操场边缘走来走去——“微型人贩子”——当场被捕。它们处于毒品链的最底层，在当地以微量而非公斤销售。

Within minutes he and his team have tracked the suspects to their door. The battering ram is out again, and soon they are in the kitchen, where a middle-aged woman protests, and a young girl bursts into tears.

几分钟之内，他和他的团队就追踪到了嫌疑人的家门口。攻城锤再次出动，很快他们就到了厨房，一名中年妇女抗议，一名年轻女孩泪流满面。

The police move from room to room, by torchlight - there's no power - opening wardrobes and lifting mattresses. Upstairs, they find a hiding place behind a window. A young suspect emerges and is detained. Two others are handcuffed in another room. All three are led away, denying that there were dealing drugs.

警察在没有电源的情况下，用手电筒从一个房间走到另一个房间，打开衣柜并举起床垫。在楼上，他们在窗户后面找到了一个藏身之处。一名年轻嫌疑人出现并被拘留。另外两个人被戴上手铐关在另一个房间里。三人均被带走，并否认参与毒品交易。

Nearby the captain finds tiny paper packets of "H" stashed in a flowerpot. It's a highly addictive mix containing small amounts of heroin sold by neighbourhood dealers for $1.00 (£0.75) per packet.

船长在附近的花盆里发现了小纸包的“H”。这是一种高度成瘾的混合物，含有少量海洛因，由附近经销商以每包 1.00 美元（0.75 英镑）的价格出售。

One of the suspects is charged, and if convicted, he could get five years in jail. Prosecutors release the other two. Senior officers say that happens a lot. They blame corruption in the justice system, and the prosecutor's office.

其中一名嫌疑人受到指控，如果罪名成立，他可能会被判处五年监禁。检察官释放了另外两人。高级官员表示，这种情况经常发生。他们指责司法系统和检察官办公室的腐败。

"We imprison them [gang members] and they release them," says Colonel Santiago Gavilanes, deputy commander of Durán police. "We know because when we carry out raids, we recapture them. Some of those who are released kill people and leave orphans behind." He tells us that the gangs have money to "buy laws, judges, and politicians".

“我们监禁他们（帮派成员），然后他们释放他们，”杜兰警察局副指挥官圣地亚哥·加维拉内斯上校说。 “我们知道，因为当我们进行突袭时，我们会重新抓获他们。其中一些被释放的人杀人并留下孤儿。”他告诉我们，帮派有钱“收买法律、法官和政客”。

We get an example of the buying power of the gangs from another police officer, in a different location. For his safety, we cannot reveal his name or where he is based.

我们从不同地点的另一名警察那里得到了团伙购买力的例子。为了他的安全，我们不能透露他的名字或他的所在地。

He told us he was contacted twice by text and offered a massive bribe - "enough money to retire on". He turned it down.

他告诉我们，有人两次通过短信联系他，并提供巨额贿赂——“足够退休的钱”。他拒绝了。

Someone – a colleague perhaps – had informed the gang exactly where he worked and what he did.

有人——也许是同事——已经准确地告诉了团伙他的工作地点和工作内容。

"You never know who is watching," he said. We heard that a lot during a week in Durán, and surrounding areas.

“你永远不知道谁在看，”他说。我们在杜兰及周边地区的一周内经常听到这样的说法。

Back at the playground it's the end of another long day in body armour, but Captain Bustamante says he has to keep going. "Maybe, I am a little tired," he admits, "but it's my work. I believe we can win this war. We have to do it."

回到操场，穿着防弹衣的漫长一天结束了，但布斯塔曼特队长说他必须继续前进。 “也许，我有点累，”他承认，“但这是我的工作。我相信我们能够赢得这场战争。我们必须这么做。”

As we speak, one of the main gang bosses in the area has been spotted nearby. "We know he controls the dealers," says the captain, "but we have no leads or evidence, so we can't arrest him".

就在我们说话的时候，该地区的一名主要帮派头目被发现在附近。 “我们知道他控制了毒贩，”船长说，“但我们没有线索或证据，所以我们无法逮捕他”。

But police here insist they are having an impact. The murder rate in Durán is down by half, they say, in the first six months of this year.

但这里的警察坚称他们正在产生影响。他们说，今年前六个月杜兰的谋杀率下降了一半。

There are other indications of progress, they tell us, with streets no longer deserted after 19:00 local time, and bodies no longer found beheaded or dismembered. That used to be common, according to Colonel Gazilanes.

他们告诉我们，还有其他进展迹象，当地时间 19:00 后街道不再空无一人，也不再发现斩首或肢解的尸体。加齐拉内斯上校表示，这种情况过去很常见。

"We have managed to contain the gangs," he says. "Getting complete control is going to be a medium-term process."

“我们已经成功遏制了这些团伙，”他说。 “获得完全控制将是一个中期过程。”

Elsewhere the criminals are containing the police. Through a contact we arranged to meet a local gang on their home turf, about an hour's drive from the city. Their leader – whom we are calling Jonathan – told me he had four officers on his payroll.

在其他地方，犯罪分子正在围堵警察。通过联系人，我们安排在当地帮派的主场与他们会面，距离城市大约一个小时的车程。他们的领导人——我们称之为乔纳森——告诉我，他的工资单上有四名军官。

"Whenever they're going to do an operation, they call me," he said. "They tell me to hide things, and to get out of here. When they arrive, they find nothing."

“每当他们要做手术时，他们都会打电话给我，”他说。 “他们告诉我把东西藏起来，然后离开这里。当他们到达时，他们什么也没发现。”

That early warning systems costs him about $1,500 (£1,100) to $2,000 (£1,500) a month.

该预警系统每月的费用约为 1,500 美元（1,100 英镑）至 2,000 美元（1,500 英镑）。

At 37, Jonathan is just a few years older than Captain Bustamante, but he's across enemy lines. He says he joined a gang, at 18, to put food on the table.

乔纳森现年 37 岁，只比布斯塔曼特上尉大几岁，但他却跨越了敌人的防线。他说，18 岁时，他加入了一个帮派，目的是为了维持生计。

He and his men (and boys) strolled through the rough-hewn streets, in broad daylight, brandishing weapons - including a semi-automatic rifle and a pistol. The youngest gang member we saw was a lean 17-year-old, wearing a bulletproof vest, and flip flops. All covered their faces.

他和他的手下（和男孩）在光天化日之下漫步在崎岖不平的街道上，挥舞着武器——包括半自动步枪和手枪。我们看到的最年轻的帮派成员是一名17岁的瘦削青年，穿着防弹背心和人字拖。 所有人都捂住了脸。

Jonathan answered our questions inside a narrow room in a breeze block house, with his lieutenants crowding in. Most of the space was taken up by a bed. He admitted to being a drug pusher and a killer. "How many lives have you taken?" I asked.

乔纳森在一座微风小屋的狭窄房间里回答了我们的问题，他的副官们挤了进来。大部分空间都被一张床占据了。他承认自己是毒贩和杀手。 “你夺走了多少人的生命？”我问。

"Well, until now about eight, I think. I don't remember well," he replied, with a handgun in his lap, pointing in my direction.

“嗯，我想到现在为止大约是八点。我不太记得了。”他回答道，膝上放着一把手枪，指着我的方向。

Jonathan claimed his gang didn't kill innocent people, just a rival criminal group trying to replace them. "They want to force their way in here," he said, "they come killing people, and we respond. The main thing for us now is to eliminate the other gang."

乔纳森声称他的团伙并没有杀害无辜者，只是一个敌对的犯罪集团试图取代他们。 “他们想强行闯入这里，”他说，“他们来这里杀人，我们就做出回应。现在我们最重要的是消灭另一个团伙。”

As we spoke, local people gathered near the doorway, children among them. Human rights workers in Ecuador told us boys can be recruited into gang life by the age of 12. Three years later they can be hitmen.

当我们说话时，当地人聚集在门口附近，其中有孩子。厄瓜多尔的人权工作者告诉我们，男孩在 12 岁时就可以被招募加入帮派生活。三年后，他们就可以成为杀手。

Jonathan makes no apologies for putting guns in the hands of teenagers or selling cocaine in the neighbourhood he claims to protect. He insists the drug trade cannot be rooted out.

乔纳森没有为将枪支交给青少年或在他声称保护的社区出售可卡因而道歉。他坚称毒品交易无法根除。

"I think it's an issue that will never end," he says. "If they shut us down, another one [gang] will come along, and if they shut that one down, there will be another."

“我认为这是一个永远不会结束的问题，”他说。 “如果他们关闭我们，另一个[团伙]就会出现，如果他们关闭那个团伙，就会出现另一个。”

He expects to die, as he has lived – by the gun. "Death is paid by death," he said. "But if they come to kill me, I won't make it easy for them."

他预计自己会死，就像他生前一样——死在枪口下。 “死亡是由死亡来偿还的，”他说。 “但如果他们来杀我，我不会让他们好过的。”

Two nights later, back in Durán, we saw how gangs settle scores. The body of a man was visible under flashing police lights, lying where he fell, beside a motorbike. Bullet casings were still on the ground. Neighbours stood looking on, mostly in silence.

两天后的晚上，回到杜兰，我们看到帮派是如何算账的。在闪烁的警灯下，可以看到一名男子的尸体，躺在他摔倒的地方，旁边是一辆摩托车。地上还残留着弹壳。邻居们站在一旁观看，大多保持沉默。

A man and woman held each other and wept – relatives of the dead man.

死者的亲属，一男一女抱在一起哭泣。

Police suspected a revenge attack – one gang against another.

警方怀疑这是一起报复性袭击——一个团伙针对另一个团伙。

Forensic teams lifted the body onto a stretcher, and into the back of a refrigerated van: violent death, on repeat. Another corpse was already inside.

法医小组将尸体抬到担架上，放入冷藏车后部：暴力死亡，重演。里面已经有另一具尸体了。

A witness was willing to speak – but not to be named. "He already had a bullet in his back," he told us. "As he fell, they finished him off with a shot to the head. He was shot six times by two men on a motorbike."

一名目击者愿意发言，但不愿透露姓名。 “他的背上已经中弹了，”他告诉我们。 “当他跌倒时，他们对着他的头部开了一枪。两名骑摩托车的男子向他开了六枪。”

"We are used to this now because it happens every day," he said. "You might just step outside and catch a stray bullet. You can't even go out or talk to friends or family because someone could shoot you."

“我们现在已经习惯了这种情况，因为这种事每天都会发生，”他说。 “你可能只是走出去，就中了一颗流弹。你甚至不能出去或与朋友或家人交谈，因为有人可能会向你开枪。”

He does not expect the president's fight against the gangs to deliver any change.

他预计总统打击帮派的行动不会带来任何改变。

"Criminals are like ghosts," he said, in a low voice. "They pop up wherever they please."

“罪犯就像幽灵一样，”他低声说道。 “他们会出现在任何他们愿意的地方。”

Then he melted away, into the warm night air.

然后他就消失了，融入夜晚温暖的空气中。

You never know who is watching.

你永远不知道谁在看。
